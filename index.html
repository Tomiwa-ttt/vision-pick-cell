    How the pipeline works
    1. An orthographic camera 1.5 m above the table renders a 256 × 256 image.
    2. Each pixel is converted to HSV. Saturated pixels are classed as red, yellow, green or blue.
    3. Connected-component labelling groups pixels into blobs and drops anything under 60 px.
    4. Blob centroid gives position. Fill ratio against the enclosing circle separates boxes from round parts, and brightness spread separates spheres from cylinders. The farthest corner gives box yaw.
    5. Closed-form inverse kinematics turns the estimate into five joint angles, with the gripper held vertical and rolled to the box faces.
    6. The grasp only holds if the estimate is within 16 mm (and 15° for boxes) of the real part.
